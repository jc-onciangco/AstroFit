The "12-3-30" workout — walk at a 12% incline, 3 mph pace, for 30 minutes — is trending again after a wave of fitness creators started posting before/after clips using the routine.

![Treadmill set to 12% incline and 3 mph](./images/12-3-30-workout.webp)

## Where it came from

The format was originally popularized a few years ago and has cycled in and out of trend feeds ever since. Its appeal is simple: no complicated programming, works on any standard treadmill, and it's low-impact enough to do daily without much recovery cost.

## What the evidence actually says

Physiologists point out that 12-3-30 is, functionally, just a structured zone 2–3 cardio session — there's nothing magic about the specific numbers. The incline raises heart rate without needing to run, which is part of why it feels more sustainable for people who dislike running.

That said, several coaches quoted in recent coverage note it works well **because** it's simple and repeatable, not because the specific 12/3/30 numbers are uniquely optimal. Swapping to 10% incline at 3.2 mph, for example, produces a very similar training effect.

## Should you try it?

If you're looking for a low-barrier way to add consistent cardio without programming decision fatigue, it's a reasonable default — just don't expect it to outperform other moderate-intensity cardio done for a similar duration.

## Quick reference

- **Incline**: 12%
- **Speed**: 3 mph
- **Duration**: 30 minutes
- **Frequency**: Most creators recommend 3–5x per week, alongside strength training
