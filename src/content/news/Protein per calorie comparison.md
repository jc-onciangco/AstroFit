Hitting a daily protein target gets a lot easier when you know which foods give you the most protein per calorie, rather than just the most protein per serving. Below is a quick reference for some of the most common sources.

## Protein density comparison

| Food (100g)              | Calories | Protein | Protein per 100 cal |
| ------------------------ | -------- | ------- | ------------------- |
| Chicken breast           | 165      | 31g     | 18.8g               |
| Egg whites               | 52       | 11g     | 21.2g               |
| Greek yogurt (nonfat)    | 59       | 10g     | 16.9g               |
| Cottage cheese (low-fat) | 98       | 11g     | 11.2g               |
| Salmon                   | 208      | 20g     | 9.6g                |
| Lentils (cooked)         | 116      | 9g      | 7.8g                |
| Almonds                  | 579      | 21g     | 3.6g                |

## Reading the table

The last column — protein per 100 calories — is the most useful number if your goal is hitting a protein target while staying within a calorie budget. Egg whites and chicken breast top the list, which is why they show up so often in high-protein meal plans.

Nuts like almonds are nutritious, but they're calorie-dense relative to their protein content, so they're a poor choice if protein-per-calorie efficiency is your main goal — better used as a fat source or snack than a primary protein source.

## Practical takeaway

If you're cutting calories and trying to preserve muscle, leaning on the top of this list (chicken breast, egg whites, Greek yogurt) will get you to your protein target with more calories left over for everything else in your day.
