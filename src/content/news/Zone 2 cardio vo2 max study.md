Researchers tracked 64 recreational athletes over 12 weeks, splitting them into two training groups: one that kept 80% of their weekly cardio volume in zone 2 (roughly 60–70% of max heart rate), and one that trained at a mix of moderate-to-high intensities.

By the end of the study, the zone 2 group showed an average VO2 max improvement of 9.4%, compared to 5.1% in the mixed-intensity group. Researchers point to improved mitochondrial density and better fat oxidation as likely drivers of the difference.

## What this means for your training

If you've been skipping "easy" cardio days in favor of higher-intensity work, this study is a reminder that low-intensity volume still plays a major role in building your aerobic base — especially over longer training blocks.

The catch: zone 2 training requires patience. Most participants in the study needed at least 6–8 weeks before seeing meaningful VO2 max changes, so it's not a quick fix.

## Key takeaways

- Zone 2 = roughly 60–70% of max heart rate, a pace where you can still hold a conversation
- Gains take time — expect at least 6 weeks before noticeable VO2 max changes
- Doesn't replace high-intensity work, but should make up the bulk of weekly cardio volume for aerobic base building
